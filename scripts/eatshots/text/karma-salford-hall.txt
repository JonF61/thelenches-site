Karma Salford Hall, The Vale of Evesham, UK

Welcome to Karma Salford Hall – a historic country retreat dating back 700 years, situated in the Vale of Evesham neighbouring the English Cotswolds, one of the prettiest rural regions anywhere in the British Isles. Nestled amid farms, fields and ancient villages, Karma Salford Hall once belonged to King Henry VIII and retains its Tudor charm while offering traditional yet contemporary comforts, including 27 charming bedrooms, restaurant, library, games room and bar. With the historic towns of Stratford Upon Avon – Shakespeare’s birthplace – and Leamington Spa – famed for its Regency architecture and shopping opportunities – Karma Salford Hall offers a quintessentially English experience that will linger long in memory.

Accommodation

Karma Salford Hall boasts 27 bedrooms. The décor celebrates the English Arts & Crafts style founded by another of the Cotswolds’ famous former residents – William Morris. Think four posters, print wallpaper, oakwood furniture and colorful upholstery. There are four room types to choose from, all en-suite and offering a choice of double, twin and four poster beds.Some rooms are pet friendly.

Cuisine

The main dining room at Karma Salford Hall, set within an elegant panelled hall, showcases a selection of local and European dishes crafted from fresh, farm-to-table ingredients. The Bar offers a cosy pub atmosphere with sofas and a fireplace, while the adjacent Beer Garden provides a relaxed outdoor setting for drinks, dining and family time. Room service and packed lunches for excursions are also available.

Tempest Restaurant

Karma Salford Hall’s Tempest Restaurant is situated in a panelled hall with period features and chandeliers. Location

Karma Salford Hall is located in the Vale of Evesham, a few miles from the famous towns of Stratford on Avon and Leamington Spa, nestled amid the rolling hills and farmland of the English Cotswolds, and designated as an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. A short tour within a 20 mile radius takes in Shakespeare’s regular stomping grounds, from Stratford on Avon, Henley-in-Arden to historic Kenilworth and Warwick.
